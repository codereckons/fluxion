\hypertarget{flx_derivation}{}\label{flx_derivation}\index{Differentiating a Program@{Differentiating a Program}}
\label{flx_derivation_md__2____w_2fluxion_2fluxion_2doc_2derivation}%
\Hypertarget{flx_derivation_md__2____w_2fluxion_2fluxion_2doc_2derivation}%


{\bfseries{FLUXION}} returns the derivatives of a C++ computation without truncation, up to order 4, mixed partial derivatives included, on scalar values and on SIMD registers alike.\hypertarget{flx_derivation_flx_program}{}\doxysubsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Programs as compositions}{Programs as compositions}}\label{flx_derivation_flx_program}
Evaluating a program on an input performs a finite sequence of elementary operations, each of them an addition, a multiplication, or a call to an elementary function. Write $v_0 = a$ for the input, $N$ for the number of operations the evaluation performs, and $\varphi_i$ for the one performed at step $i$. Each step reads the values already computed, and the last one is the result:\+

\[ v_i = \varphi_i(v_0, \dots, v_{i-1}) \quad (1 \le i \le N), \qquad f(a) = v_N. \]

A program is therefore a composition, and differentiating what it computes requires two things:\+ the derivative of each elementary operation, and a rule giving the derivative of a composition from the derivatives of its parts.\hypertarget{flx_derivation_flx_derivative}{}\doxysubsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Derivative at a point}{Derivative at a point}}\label{flx_derivation_flx_derivative}
For $f$ defined around $a$, the derivative at $a$ is the limit of the difference quotient,

\[ f\textnormal{\textquotesingle}(a) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h}, \]

when that limit exists. Written as a Taylor expansion, the same statement reads

\[ f(a+h) = f(a) + f\textnormal{\textquotesingle}(a)\,h + o(h). \]

A derivative is a number at a point; a formula for $f\textnormal{\textquotesingle}$ is one way of computing it.

For an elementary operation, that formula is known in closed form, for instance

\[ (x^n)\textnormal{\textquotesingle} = n\,x^{n-1}, \qquad (\sin x)\textnormal{\textquotesingle} = \cos x, \qquad (e^x)\textnormal{\textquotesingle} = e^x, \qquad
    (\log |x|)\textnormal{\textquotesingle} = \frac{1}{x}. \]

Every differentiable $\varphi_i$ of a program has such a formula, which settles the first of the two requirements.\hypertarget{flx_derivation_flx_rules}{}\doxysubsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Rules of computation}{Rules of computation}}\label{flx_derivation_flx_rules}
Four rules give the derivative of a compound expression from the derivatives of its parts:\+

\[ (u+v)\textnormal{\textquotesingle} = u\textnormal{\textquotesingle} + v\textnormal{\textquotesingle}, \qquad
    (uv)\textnormal{\textquotesingle}   = u\textnormal{\textquotesingle}v + uv\textnormal{\textquotesingle}, \qquad
    \left(\frac{u}{v}\right)\textnormal{\textquotesingle} = \frac{u\textnormal{\textquotesingle}v - uv\textnormal{\textquotesingle}}{v^2}, \qquad
    (f \circ g)\textnormal{\textquotesingle}(a) = f\textnormal{\textquotesingle}\big(g(a)\big)\,g\textnormal{\textquotesingle}(a). \]

The last of them settles the second requirement. It gives $(f \circ g)\textnormal{\textquotesingle}(a)$ from $g\textnormal{\textquotesingle}(a)$ and from $f\textnormal{\textquotesingle}$ taken at $g(a)$, two values of functions known in closed form, at the points the evaluation of $f\big(g(a)\big)$ already computes.\hypertarget{flx_derivation_flx_carry}{}\doxysubsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Propagating derivatives}{Propagating derivatives}}\label{flx_derivation_flx_carry}
Applied to the \doxysectlink{flx_derivation_flx_program}{program}{1} sequence once per operation, the chain rule produces a second sequence beside it,

\[ d_0 = 1, \qquad
    d_i = \sum_{j<i} \frac{\partial \varphi_i}{\partial v_j}(v_0,\dots,v_{i-1})\, d_j, \]

with $d_N = f\textnormal{\textquotesingle}(a)$. Every partial derivative in the sum is that of a single elementary operation, taken from its closed form, and the sum has as many terms as that operation has arguments, so two at most for the arithmetic operations.

The two sequences are computed together, $d_i$ needing the operands of $v_i$ and the $d_j$ already obtained. Each value therefore carries its own derivative, $f\textnormal{\textquotesingle}(a)$ is available when the program returns, and no expression for $f\textnormal{\textquotesingle}$ is written anywhere.

The derivative is that of the sequence of operations performed on this input. A branch on a comparison, a loop whose count depends on the data, a value read from a table:\+ the execution selects one path, and the result is the derivative along that path. {\ttfamily x \texorpdfstring{$<$}{<} 1 ? x :\+ 2 -\/ x} returns the derivative $-1$ at $x = 1$, that of the branch taken, at a point where the function it computes has no derivative.\hypertarget{flx_derivation_flx_higher}{}\doxysubsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Higher orders and several arguments}{Higher orders and several arguments}}\label{flx_derivation_flx_higher}
Differentiating $f\textnormal{\textquotesingle}$ gives $f\textnormal{\textquotesingle}\textnormal{\textquotesingle}$, and differentiation repeats as long as the result stays differentiable. For a function of $m$ arguments, the partial derivative $\partial f/\partial x_j$ is the derivative of the function of $x_j$ alone obtained by holding the other arguments fixed. Two objects are built from these:\+


\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item the {\bfseries{gradient}}, the $m$ first partial derivatives at the point;
\item the {\bfseries{Hessian}}, the $m^2$ second ones, of which $m(m+1)/2$ are distinct when the second partials are continuous, since $\partial^2 f/\partial x\,\partial y$ and $\partial^2 f/\partial y\,\partial x$ agree there.
\end{DoxyItemize}

Optimization and the solution of nonlinear systems use the gradient at a point, a second-\/order method uses the Hessian as well, and an iterative method requires both at every step. Each is a set of numbers carried through the same evaluation, and the count of those numbers sets the size of the objects the arithmetic operates on.

{\bfseries{FLUXION}} holds a value and the requested derivatives in one object, so the second sequence is computed by the arithmetic itself.
