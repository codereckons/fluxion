\hypertarget{flx_methods}{}\label{flx_methods}\index{Obtaining a Derivative@{Obtaining a Derivative}}
\label{flx_methods_md__2____w_2fluxion_2fluxion_2doc_2methods}%
\Hypertarget{flx_methods_md__2____w_2fluxion_2fluxion_2doc_2methods}%


Five methods compute a derivative. They differ in their accuracy, their cost, and what they require of the code.\hypertarget{flx_methods_flx_symbolic}{}\doxysubsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Symbolic differentiation}{Symbolic differentiation}}\label{flx_methods_flx_symbolic}
Differentiating a formula gives an exact expression, and evaluating it gives a value exact up to rounding. It requires a formula:\+ a program with a loop, a branch, or a call into a library is not one. It also forms the composed expression that \doxysectlink{flx_derivation_flx_rules}{Rules of computation}{1} avoids, and repeated differentiation makes that expression grow, each order multiplying the number of terms.\hypertarget{flx_methods_flx_automatic}{}\doxysubsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Automatic differentiation}{Automatic differentiation}}\label{flx_methods_flx_automatic}
Both modes apply the chain rule to the sequence of operations the program performed, so neither has a truncation error:\+ a derivative is rounded as the value it accompanies is.


\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item {\bfseries{Forward mode}}, which {\bfseries{FLUXION}} implements, carries the derivatives alongside the value. The cost of one evaluation grows with the number of components carried, and nothing is stored between evaluations. A gradient with respect to $m$ arguments requires a number of passes that grows with $m$.
\item {\bfseries{Reverse mode}} records the operations of one evaluation, then traverses the record backward. A gradient with respect to $m$ arguments requires one forward pass and one backward pass for any $m$, and the record grows with the number of operations executed.
\end{DoxyItemize}

Forward mode suits few inputs or high orders, reverse mode a single output differentiated with respect to many inputs.\hypertarget{flx_methods_flx_quotient}{}\doxysubsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Difference quotient}{Difference quotient}}\label{flx_methods_flx_quotient}
For a small $h$, the forward difference contains two error terms at once:\+

\[ \frac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h} = f\textnormal{\textquotesingle}(a) + \frac{h}{2}f\textnormal{\textquotesingle}\textnormal{\textquotesingle}(\xi)
    + O\!\left(\frac{u\,|f(a)|}{h}\right), \]

with $u$ the unit roundoff. The truncation error grows with $h$ and the cancellation with $1/h$, so their sum is smallest near $h = \sqrt{u}$, which leaves about half of the available digits. A central difference reduces the truncation error and leaves the cancellation unchanged. A second derivative divides by $h^2$:\+ its central difference keeps about half of the digits, its one-\/sided difference about a third.

No choice of $h$ removes both errors, and the digits the cancellation removes cannot be restored afterward.

The difference quotient is also the only method that does not apply the chain rule:\+ it evaluates the program twice and takes the difference.\hypertarget{flx_methods_flx_complex}{}\doxysubsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Complex step}{Complex step}}\label{flx_methods_flx_complex}
Lyness and Moler (1967) and Squire and Trapp (1998) show that $\Im\big(f(a + ih)\big)/h$ approximates $f\textnormal{\textquotesingle}(a)$ with no subtraction of nearby values. The cancellation disappears, $h$ may be taken as small as the representation allows, and the first derivative is obtained to full precision.

It gives the first derivative only. The second appears in the real part, $\Re\big(f(a + ih)\big) = f(a) - \frac{h^2}{2}f\textnormal{\textquotesingle}\textnormal{\textquotesingle}(a) + O(h^4)$, and recovering it subtracts $f(a)$ and reintroduces the cancellation.

The method also requires:\+


\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item a real analytic function;
\item an implementation that accepts complex arguments and stays analytic through every branch it takes.
\end{DoxyItemize}\hypertarget{flx_methods_flx_summary}{}\doxysubsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Comparison of methods}{Comparison of methods}}\label{flx_methods_flx_summary}
